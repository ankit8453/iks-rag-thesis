# 2026-06-28

## Symptom-driven retrieval implemented (the approved redesign)

**Why.** Our expert Dr. Sunita T. Pandey said the classical texts are **general and symptom-based, not crop-specific** — a remedy given for a symptom applies across plants showing it (her kunapajala work was validated on potato among many crops). Deep web research confirmed it (24/25 claims verified, 3-vote adversarial): Vrikshayurveda classifies affliction by symptom + dosha, not by crop, and a peer-reviewed Vrikshayurveda expert system diagnoses purely from symptom questions and never asks the crop. Dr. Akshay Pandey approved the change.

**The flaw this fixes.** Our pipeline was effectively treating the corpus as a *crop-indexed database*: the query led with the plant name, and when that name wasn't in the texts the generator refused ("not enough information"). That is unfaithful to how these texts actually work, and it was *under-reporting* real coverage (e.g. corn/potato refusals that shouldn't have been refusals).

**Two changes, both faithful to the sources:**
1. `src/integration/strategy_llm_mediated.py` — Strategy B now states up front that the treatises are general/symptom-based, adds a **"LEAD WITH THE SYMPTOM"** rule (crop may appear once, late; never as the subject; the crop-led anti-patterns "what does <crop> disease look like" / "remedies for <crop>" are explicitly banned as poor-retrieval), and the structured context demotes the crop to *"background context only — the texts are not crop-specific"*. The detected disease label is still carried and translated into its symptom family (the earlier parroting fix stands).
2. `src/rag/generator.py` — added **rule 1a** to the §17 grounded-advisor prompt. The faithfulness guardrail is untouched; what changed is its *scope*: a passage addressing the observed condition now counts as sufficient evidence **even if it never names the crop**, so the model must not refuse merely because the crop is absent. It refuses only when no passage addresses the condition at all, and must state plainly when leaning on a general passage.

**Nuance kept honest.** Surapala does contain a minority of crop-named recipes (mango, pomegranate, coconut), so the framing is "predominantly symptom-general", never an absolute claim.

**Validated.** New `tests/integration/test_symptom_driven.py` (7 tests) locks in: symptom-led framing, the lead-with-symptom rule, crop demotion, disease label retained, the refusal guardrail still present, refusal correctly scoped, and honest flagging of general passages. **Full suite: 314 passed.**
