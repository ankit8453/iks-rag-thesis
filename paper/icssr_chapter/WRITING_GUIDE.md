## 2. A book chapter is NOT a journal paper

What the research says, in simple words:

- **A journal paper** says: *"here is my method, here are my numbers, here is my contribution."*
  Heavy on method and results.
- **A book chapter** says: *"here is how my work helps this book's big theme."*
  Our book's theme is **Indigenous Knowledge Systems, cultural continuity, digital
  preservation**. So the chapter must keep asking: *what does this work mean for IKS?*

What that means for us:

1. **Less technical detail.** Readers are social scientists and IKS scholars, not AI
   engineers. No equations. Explain every technical idea in one plain sentence. Full details
   can be in the thesis — you can say *"details are reported in the thesis"*.
2. **More context and meaning.** Spend words on *why* the texts are hard to use, *what* the
   gap between the two traditions means, *why* refusing is better than inventing.
3. **Same work, different balance.** We are not inventing new results for the chapter. Same
   findings, told for a different reader.

---

## 3. "Revised and extended" — it must contain new material

For extended versions, publishers generally expect **about 25–30% new material** — and it
must be new *ideas*, not just more pages. It is also normal to **write a fresh
introduction** rather than reuse the old one.

We have plenty that was NOT in the abstract or the presentation:
- the **coverage finding** (texts index disease by cause, modern diagnosis by appearance —
  4 of 22 disease questions truly answerable)
- the **bridge measurement** (Llama's queries were 3.8× worse than human wording, fixed to
  level by prompt design)
- the **6% vs 60%** result (disease-name questions vs ordinary farming questions)
- **lessons from digitising** the texts (OCR truncation, chapter over-capture)
- **Vishvavallabha** added as the sixth text

Mention in the chapter that it builds on the paper presented at the seminar.

## 4. The AI rule — what is actually allowed

Researched the big publishers (Taylor & Francis/Routledge, Springer, Elsevier, Wiley, SAGE).
They all say roughly the same:

- ✅ **Allowed:** using AI for grammar, language improvement, translation, ideas —
  **but you must declare it** (tool name, version, what it was used for).
- ❌ **Not allowed:** submitting AI-written text without real human rewriting.
- ❌ AI can never be an author.

**Our method (safe under every policy):**
- I give you **bullet points with facts and numbers** (SKELETON.md).
- **You write the sentences yourself.**
- I check your draft only for **wrong facts or missing points**. You fix it yourself.
- At the end we add a short honest AI statement, e.g. *"An AI assistant (Claude) was used to
  organise results and check factual consistency; all text was written by the authors."*

## 6. Plagiarism and AI checks

- Ask the **IIITDM library** for a **DrillBit or Turnitin** report — most Indian institutes
  have one of these. Aim for **under 10% similarity**.
- Your own seminar abstract may show up as a match. That is normally fine (it is your own
  work), but if the % is high, re-word those lines.
- Run both checks **around 20–23 Oct**, not on the last day — leave time to rewrite flagged
  parts.
- Quotes from the texts (e.g. a Vrikshayurveda verse) must be in quotation marks with a
  citation — then they don't count as plagiarism.

## 7. APA references — the only formats we need

- **Book:** Sadhale, N. (Trans.). (1996). *Surapala's Vrikshayurveda (The science of plant life
  by Surapala)* (Agri-History Bulletin No. 1). Asian Agri-History Foundation.
- **Book chapter:** Author, A. A. (Year). Title of chapter. In E. Editor (Ed.), *Title of book*
  (pp. xx–xx). Publisher.
- **Journal article:** Author, A. A., & Author, B. B. (Year). Title of article. *Journal Name,
  Volume*(Issue), pages. https://doi.org/...
- **arXiv / preprint:** Author, A. A. (Year). *Title* (arXiv:xxxx.xxxxx). arXiv.
  https://doi.org/...
- **Conference paper:** Author, A. A. (Year). Title. In *Proceedings of ...* (pp. xx–xx).
  Publisher.
- **Software / model:** Meta AI. (2024). *Llama 3.1 8B Instruct* [Large language model].
  https://...

Rules: only the first word + names capitalised in titles; book and journal names in
*italics*; in-text citation looks like **(Sadhale, 1996)** or **Sadhale (1996) says...**

## 8. Writing tips that matter most

- **One idea per paragraph.** First sentence says the idea, rest supports it.
- **Short sentences.** If a sentence has more than two commas, split it.
- **Explain every technical word the first time**: *"retrieval — finding the right passage"*.
- **Honesty is our strength.** Say plainly what didn't work. This audience will respect
  "the texts cannot answer this" far more than an inflated number.
- **Don't polish while drafting.** Write rough first, fix later.
- **Speak it if typing is slow**: Google Docs → Tools → Voice typing. Works in English and
  Hindi. Then clean it up.
